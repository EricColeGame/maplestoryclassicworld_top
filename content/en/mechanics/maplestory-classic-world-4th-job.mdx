Level 120 is the wall that separates casual players from committed ones, and the MapleStory Classic World 4th job advancement is what waits on the other side of it. Everything you have been building toward — your class line, your gear, your Meso stash — gets multiplied the moment you finish that quest chain. This guide covers the requirements, the quest steps, the Mastery Book grind, and the SP decisions that decide whether your MapleStory Classic World 4th job character feels powerful or permanently behind.

## Why the 4th Job Advancement Is the Real Endgame Gate

In the classic ruleset there is no 5th job, no Hyper Skills, no V Matrix, and no army of link-skill alts feeding your main. The 4th job is the final job tier and level 200 is the hard ceiling. That means a player who reaches 120 has roughly 80 more levels to spend inside a single skill tree. The MapleStory Classic World 4th job is less a milestone and more a long-term project.

The math is straightforward. You earn 3 skill points per level after 120, so a character who goes all the way to 200 has around 240 SP to pour into 4th job skills — and most of those skills cap at 20 or 30 points. You cannot max everything. That single constraint is why build planning matters far more here than at any earlier tier.

The payoff is equally clear. Fourth-job skills are the ones that carry boss runs, and they include the party buffs that make you genuinely desirable in a group. Before 120, you are leveling a character. After 120, you are building one.

| Milestone | Level | What it means in the classic ruleset |
|---|---|---|
| 1st job advancement | 10 (8 for Magicians) | Basic class identity and first attack skills |
| 2nd job advancement | 30 | Your specialization branch opens up |
| 3rd job advancement | 70 | Mid-game kit, first serious mobbing tools |
| 4th job advancement | 120 | Final job tier; unlocks the endgame skill tree |
| Level cap | 200 | Roughly 80 levels of 4th job SP to spend |

## Prerequisites: Level 120, Instructor Quests, and Heroic Items

The gate itself is simple: your character must be level 120 and must have already completed the 3rd job advancement. You cannot skip ahead, and you cannot bank the quest early.

From there, the chain runs through your job instructor. In the original classic-era version of the quest, you are asked to bring back two rare quest items: a Heroic Star and a Heroic Pentagon. Community reports consistently point to the field bosses Manon and Griffey in the Leafre region as the sources. Some classic-style servers adjust the exact requirement, so always confirm the current text in your in-game quest log rather than trusting a decade-old guide.

What has not changed is the friction. These are field bosses, not instanced encounters, which means you are competing with other players for spawns and channels. Player experience suggests treating this step as a multi-session project rather than an evening's work.

| Requirement | Details | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Character level | 120 minimum | Hard gate; no workaround exists |
| 3rd job completed | Must be finished | The chain will not start otherwise |
| Job instructor quest | Started from your class NPC | Begins the advancement chain |
| Heroic Star | Rare drop from a Leafre field boss | Often the longer half of the hunt |
| Heroic Pentagon | Rare drop from a second field boss | Plan channels and party help in advance |
| Free inventory space | At least a few open slots | Quest items can be missed if your bag is full |

## Step-by-Step Through the 4th Job Advancement

The order below reflects how the classic chain generally plays out. Treat it as a checklist and confirm each step in your quest log as you go.

1. **Reach level 120.** Finish any pending 3rd job quests first, since an unfinished quest can block the new chain.
2. **Talk to your job instructor.** The quest notification usually appears automatically, but walking up to the NPC yourself is faster than waiting.
3. **Accept the advancement quest.** Read the quest text carefully — this is where the required items are listed.
4. **Hunt the field bosses for the quest items.** Bring potions, expect a crowd, and consider asking a higher-level friend to help hold a channel.
5. **Return and turn in the items.** The instructor completes the advancement and you officially become a 4th job character.
6. **Rebuild your keyboard layout.** Your new skills arrive unassigned, and the first thing most players do is fumble a boss fight because their old muscle memory is now wrong.

| Step | Action | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hit level 120 | Clear old quests that may block the chain |
| 2 | Visit your job instructor | Quest alerts can be easy to miss during grinding |
| 3 | Accept the chain | Note the exact item names in your log |
| 4 | Farm the quest items | Contested spawns; use party play and channel switching |
| 5 | Turn in and advance | Your job title changes immediately |
| 6 | Assign new skills | Do this before your next boss run, not during it |

## Mastery Books: The Hidden Cost of 4th Job Skills

This is the part new players underestimate. Your shiny new 4th job skills do not arrive at full power. In the classic system, most of them start with a low skill-point cap of 10, and you must consume a Mastery Book to raise that cap. A Mastery Book 20 lifts the ceiling to 20; a Mastery Book 30 lifts it to 30. Books are tied to specific skills, which is why the market for a single popular book can be brutal.

Because books are tradeable, they become one of the biggest Meso sinks in the game. Community reports describe certain books as effectively a second grind layered on top of the level grind. If you are budgeting, decide early whether you would rather farm your books yourself or farm Mesos and buy them.

A few 4th job tools sit outside this system entirely. Utility skills such as the status-clearing Hero's Will are typically unlocked through a quest rather than a book, and the universal party buff Maple Warrior is one of the skills nearly every class wants to invest in. Check each skill's tooltip to see whether a book is required before you spend SP on it.

| Book tier | Effect | Typical source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| No book required | Skill can be maxed directly | Quest reward or innate | Usually utility skills |
| Mastery Book 20 | Raises skill cap from 10 to 20 | Boss drops, some quests | Entry point for most 4th job skills |
| Mastery Book 30 | Raises skill cap from 20 to 30 | Rarer boss drops | Usually the expensive purchase |
| Skill-specific books | Each book unlocks one skill | Drops and player market | Prices vary wildly by class popularity |

| Situation | Recommended approach | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| You play a popular class | Buy your key books early | Prices rise as the server matures |
| You play a niche class | Farm and buy opportunistically | Book supply can be thin and unpredictable |
| You are short on Mesos | Prioritize one core attack skill | A single maxed skill beats four half-built ones |
| You have a guild | Ask about shared book drops | Player experience: guilds often pool rare books |

## SP Planning, Build Priorities, and Common Mistakes

You get 3 SP per level from 120 onward, which sounds generous until you realize how many skills want those points. The reliable framework is to fund your damage first, your party value second, and your comfort skills last.

Your primary attack skill should be the first thing you push toward its cap, because it is the skill you will press thousands of times. After that, prioritize anything that multiplies the whole party's output, then survivability passives, then mobility and quality-of-life tools. Utility skills that solve a specific problem — like removing a crippling status effect — are worth a single point early even if you max them much later.

| Priority | What to fund first | Examples from common builds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core bossing attack | Brandish, Blast, Berserk, Hurricane, Triple Throw |
| 2 | Party damage buffs | Sharp Eyes, Maple Warrior, Time Leap |
| 3 | Survivability passives | Achilles, Guardian, Beholder |
| 4 | Mobility and utility | Rush, Smoke Screen, Hero's Will |

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spending SP before buying books | Points sit uselessly under a low cap | Confirm book availability first |
| Spreading SP across many skills | Nothing is strong enough for boss content | Concentrate on one skill at a time |
| Skipping party buffs | You become a solo-only character | Fund the buff that helps everyone |
| Ignoring quest-gated skills | You miss free utility with no book cost | Finish class quests right after advancing |
| Buying the rarest book first | You overspend on a skill you rarely use | Match purchases to your actual rotation |

## FAQ

### What level do you need for the 4th job in MapleStory Classic World?

You need to reach level 120 and have completed the 3rd job advancement. There is no shortcut, no quest item that bypasses the level requirement, and no way to start the chain early. Once you hit 120, the advancement quest becomes available through your job instructor.

### Can I choose a different 4th job than my class line suggests?

No. The MapleStory Classic World 4th job is determined entirely by your 3rd job branch. A Crusader becomes a Hero, a Priest becomes a Bishop, and a Hermit becomes a Night Lord. If you want a different endgame class, you need to level a new character rather than expect a choice at 120.

### Are Mastery Books mandatory to max my skills?

For most 4th job skills, yes. Without the appropriate book, your skill point cap stays low and you cannot reach the maximum level on that skill. Some utility skills are quest-gated instead, so read each skill's requirements carefully before assuming you need to buy anything.

### What should I do immediately after advancing?

Assign your new skills to your keyboard, buy or farm the book for your core attack skill, and finish any class quests that unlock free utility skills. Then test your new rotation on a familiar boss before joining a serious party run. Community reports suggest most players lose their first post-advancement boss fight purely because they never rehearsed the new skill order.
